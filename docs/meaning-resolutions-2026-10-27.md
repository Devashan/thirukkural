## Chosen meanings

### 543

The king's just rule is the foundation of the Brahmins' sacred texts and the virtue they teach.

Follow Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar: அந்தணர் நூல் refers here to the Vedas associated with Brahmins. Retain that historical identification rather than silently generalising it to all sages.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/543 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 550

A king putting cruel wrongdoers to death is like a farmer removing weeds to protect a growing crop.

Both commentaries explicitly take கொலையின் as punishment by death, not a comparison with killers. Preserve the historical claim and crop simile.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/550 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 614

The wish to help others, without sustained effort, fails like a sword in the hands of someone afraid to fight.

Use Manakkudavar's functional explanation of பேடி as one afraid on seeing an army. The historical gendered term, rendered 'eunuch' by Aiyar, is interpreted through its role in the simile; this is an idiomatic explanation, not a literal equivalence or an identification with a modern gender group.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/614 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 727

The learning of someone afraid to speak in an assembly is like a gleaming sword held by someone afraid to fight on the battlefield.

Use Manakkudavar's explicit parallel between fear in battle and fear in an assembly, consistently with 614. பேடி is a historical gendered term; the idiomatic explanation makes the function of the image explicit rather than reproducing that social label.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/727 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 808

For those who will not listen to accusations against an old friend, a day when that friend wrongs them is a chance to show the strength of their friendship.

Both commentaries treat நாள் as a useful or good day. Parimelalagar explains that the friend's fault gives an occasion to demonstrate forbearance; do not turn நாளிழுக்கம் into blaming the day.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/808 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 829

When people lavish friendship on you while despising you within, keep up a friendly face, but let the friendship die in your heart.

Follow both commentaries: it is the inward friendship that dies, not the false friend who is crushed. The outward courtesy and inward withdrawal are interpretive choices explicitly supported by the glosses.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/829 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 918

They say the embrace of deceitful women is like a spirit that beguiles and destroys those who lack discernment.

Follow Parimelalagar's explanation of அணங்கு as a female divinity that takes life through desire: apparent pleasure followed by ruin. Keep both allure and harm, rather than choosing one alone; no Greek 'siren' is introduced.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/918 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 943

Once your previous meal is digested, eat an amount you know you can digest: that is the way to preserve your body for a long life.

Both commentaries explain knowing the amount that can be digested. The source verse's அறவறிந்து is retained unchanged; the commentary's expanded அளவு அறிந்து supports moderation, not omission of the quantity condition.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/943 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1074

When base people see others behaving more basely than themselves, they swell with pride at their own supposed superiority.

Choose the comparison with people below oneself in conduct, supported by Parimelalagar's gloss of அகப்பட்டி and the prose explanation alongside Pope. Do not describe a competition to commit worse acts. Other readings remain recorded in the prior snapshot.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1074 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1172

These kohl-lined eyes looked at him without thinking of the consequences; why do they grieve now, instead of recognising that they must bear the sorrow they brought on themselves?

Follow Parimelalagar's explicit explanation of பரிந்து உணரா: failing to recognise that this self-caused suffering must be borne. This supplies the commentary basis missing from the earlier review; other commentarial readings exist.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1172 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1178

He is here, the man whose words professed love while his heart did not; yet my eyes find no peace without seeing him.

Follow Parimelalagar: the lover is the subject of பேணாது பெட்டார், with verbal affection contrasted with inward love. Do not turn this into a general claim about unrequited lovers.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1178 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1189

Let my body grow pale as it will, if the man who persuaded me to accept his leaving is to be held blameless.

Choose Parimelalagar's reproachful reading: நன்னிலையர் concerns standing in goodness or being without fault, not simply physical welfare. His gloss links நயப்பித்தார் to obtaining her consent to separation. The welfare reading remains an alternative, not an error asserted to be settled for all editions.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1189 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1231

Thinking of him who went far away and left me in sorrow, my eyes have lost their lustre and are ashamed before the fragrant flowers.

Choose Parimelalagar's reading that sorrow remains with the speaker, supported by Pope's rendering. Manakkudavar and Aiyar instead read the departure as intended to end distress or want. Loss of lustre explains the flower comparison in the chosen commentary; no change to the Tamil is made.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1231 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1262

My bright-jewelled friend, if I forget him even today, my arms will lose their beauty and their bangles will slip away.

Both commentaries take மறப்பின் as a condition with ensuing loss, not a statement that loss has already happened. Preserve the address to the friend and the conditional tense. Parimelalagar's additional next-life interpretation is not required by Manakkudavar and is not inserted into this idiomatic draft.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1262 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).

### 1306

Without lovers' quarrels, both deep and brief, love is like fruit that is overripe or still unripe.

Both commentaries treat அற்று as a comparison. Manakkudavar explicitly describes the spoiled and unripe fruit; do not render this as love lacking its fruit. துனி and புலவி distinguish prolonged and lesser quarrels.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/1306 (Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar sections).
