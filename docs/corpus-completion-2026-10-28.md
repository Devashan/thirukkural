## Meanings and decisions

### 37

There is no need to explain the fruits of virtue in words: look at the difference between the person carrying a palanquin and the person riding in it.

Follow Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar, who take the image as visible evidence of virtue's fruit. The alternative prohibitive reading remains in the preceding snapshot. This is a historical karmic comparison, not a claim that present social rank establishes a person's moral worth.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/37 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.

### 184

Even if you speak harshly to someone's face, do not speak behind their back without considering the harm your words may cause.

Follow Parimelalagar's reading of the speaker and of பின் as ensuing harm. Manakkudavar also puts the harsh speech in the speaker's mouth, but explains the ending through inability to face the person later. Aiyar instead makes the other person the harsh speaker.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/184 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.

### 223

A person of noble birth gives to those in need without turning them away with the painful words, 'I have nothing.'

Follow Manakkudavar's explicit giver-as-speaker reading, consistent with Aiyar. Parimelalagar's main gloss distinguishes not begging oneself and giving to others; he also records giving before a request and giving without pleading poverty as alternatives. Retain the verse's historical birth-language rather than silently changing it into a claim only about character.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/223 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.

### 268

All other living beings worship the one who, through austerity, has become master of his own life.

Follow Parimelalagar's explanation of making one's life one's own through the work of austerity, consistent with Pope's self-mastery reading. Manakkudavar instead explicitly stresses the ending of ego. Retain worship and all living beings; do not claim the two glosses are identical.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/268 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.

### 329

Those who make killing their work are regarded as base in conduct by people who understand its baseness.

Render the moral judgement attached to the occupation, as in Pope, rather than Aiyar's carrion-eating image. Both medieval glosses use historical caste-marked language for புலைவினையர், which is documented here rather than mapped to a modern community in this idiomatic explanation. The judgement concerns conduct; this is not a literal translation of the social label.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/329 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.

### 354

Even knowing all that the five senses can reveal brings no benefit without understanding the true nature of things.

Follow Manakkudavar's explicit knowledge-through-the-five-senses reading, consistent with Pope. Parimelalagar instead explains concentrated mastery of the fivefold mind. Record this alternative rather than presenting mastery and sensory knowledge as the same reading.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/354 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.

### 388

A king who rules justly and protects his people will be regarded by them as a god.

Follow Parimelalagar's explicit கடவுள் gloss: a human ruler is elevated through his deeds. Manakkudavar instead uses leader or lord. Lower-case 'a god' preserves the comparison without importing a unique deity or asserting literal divinity.

Source: https://thirukkural.io/kural/388 — Parimelalagar and Manakkudavar transcriptions, compared 2026-10-28. No scan verification claimed.
